Part 1 · Section 01

System Ownership Model

Four systems, one source of truth. HubSpot, MonetizeNow, Stripe, QuickBooks. This page covers what each one is responsible for, what it isn't, and where MN sits in the middle.

◆
How to read this page. HubSpot owns pipeline intent. MonetizeNow owns commercial truth. Stripe is the payment rail. QuickBooks is the ledger. Anything that affects what was sold, what is owed, or what should be billed goes into MonetizeNow first.
H
HubSpot
Pipeline Intent
Owns
  • Opportunity lifecycle and deal stages
  • Account and contact records
  • Sales rep activity and forecast
  • Customer-facing rep workflow (where reps actually live)
Does not own
  • Pricing or quote configuration
  • Billing dates or schedules
  • Contract state (active, amended, renewed)
"Is this deal real, and what stage is it in?"
M
MonetizeNow
Commercial Source of Truth
Owns
  • Product catalog, offerings, rate cards
  • Quote configuration, approvals, discounts
  • Contract state, amendments, renewals
  • Invoice generation, billing schedules, proration
  • Subscription state and rating
  • Payment orchestration (instructions to Stripe)
  • Revenue data: ARR, MRR, contract value, billed amounts
Does not own
  • Pipeline forecast (that lives in HubSpot)
  • GL posting or revenue recognition entries (QBO)
  • Money movement itself (Stripe)
"What exactly did we sell, what does the customer owe, and when?"
S
Stripe
Payment Rail
Owns
  • Card processing and ACH
  • Payment method storage (tokens)
  • Charge attempts, declines, retries (per MN instruction)
  • Refund execution and chargeback handling
Does not own
  • Invoice generation or billing logic
  • Customer record of truth
  • What to retry, when to retry, or how many times
"Move the money exactly as MN told me to."
Q
QuickBooks
Accounting Ledger
Owns
  • General ledger and chart of accounts
  • Revenue recognition entries
  • Financial statements: P&L, balance sheet
  • Tax filing and reporting
  • Bank reconciliation
Does not own (today)
  • Invoice generation (MN does it)
  • Customer billing relationship
  • Payment collection workflow
"How do we close the books on this revenue?"
Why MonetizeNow is the source of truth, not HubSpot or Stripe
A reasonable question. customer-facing record exists in HubSpot. The money lives in Stripe. So why does MN get to be authoritative?
HubSpot tells you a deal exists
It does not know what was actually agreed to. rep can change Deal Amount with a typo. The pricing logic lives elsewhere.
Stripe tells you money moved
It does not know what the charge was for, what the term is, what the renewal looks like, or how to prorate the next change. Stripe is a rail, not a record.
MN ties everything together
It carries the contract, the schedule, the proration rules, and the reference back to both HubSpot Deal and Stripe charge. Audit trail starts and ends here.
▲
Fixing data: pricing, dates, or contract terms wrong, fix in MonetizeNow. Pipeline stage wrong, fix in HubSpot. Revenue recognition or journal entry wrong, fix in QuickBooks. Edits in the wrong system create drift that surfaces at the next close.

What does not sync back

Part 2 · Section 04 · Motion 1 of 3

New Business Motion

From a fresh opportunity to a paid first invoice. No prior context, no existing contract to honor. Every value is set from scratch and every downstream record is created at signature.

◆
How that chain works. An Opportunity anchors a Quote. A Quote anchors a Contract. A Contract anchors Subscriptions. Subscriptions generate Invoices. Invoices charge through Stripe and surface in Finance reports for QBO posting. Reference IDs flow through every link, which is how an invoice three months from now can be traced back to a specific Quote.

What runs automatically
  • HubSpot Deal Amount update populates from Quote ARR or TCV on every status change.
  • Quote status → Deal stage Draft maps to Qualification, Sent maps to Negotiation, Processed maps to Closed Won.
  • Contract creation fires on signature, no manual step.
  • Subscription activation one Subscription per Quote Item, all activated at Contract start.
  • Invoice schedule built from billing frequency on the Rate. Runs nightly or immediately depending on configuration.
  • Stripe charge fires on invoice post if Auto-Charge is on for the customer.
  • MN reports, populate automatically as invoices post. Finance pulls them at close to drive QBO entries.
  • HubSpot Contract ID stamp written back to Deal so CS and AM can see the active state.
What needs a human
  • Configure Quote pick the Offering, add Products, set quantities. Reps do this.
  • Approve a discount if over the threshold, a manager approves in MN.
  • Mark winning Quote as Primary if multiple Quotes were created, rep must flag the one that was actually signed.
  • Send Quote for signature explicit action, not automatic.
  • Process the signed Quote review and click Process. Locks pricing and creates Contract.
  • Confirm payment method on file for first-time customers, customer adds a card via a hosted page before the first charge.

Part 2 · Section 05 · Motion 2 of 3

Amendment Motion

A change to an active contract before its term ends. More seats, a new product line, a downgrade, a quantity swap. Quote calculates a delta against the existing contract, invoice is a proration, contract updates in place.

▲
Most common error. Building an amendment as a brand-new quote instead of amending the existing contract. A second contract runs in parallel and the customer gets billed twice. Amendments start from active Contract in MN.
Amendment vs New Business
DimensionNew BusinessAmendment
Starting pointA new Opportunity in HubSpot. No prior commercial context.An active Contract in MN. Existing terms and billing dates already in motion.
Quote purposeDefine the full commercial agreement from scratch.Define only what is changing. Adds, removes, quantity shifts.
Billing impactSets the initial billing schedule. New anchor date.Proration calculated from Effective Date against the existing anchor.
Forecast in HubSpotFull TCV / ARR.Incremental ARR only. Otherwise pipeline is double-counted.
Contract resultNew Contract record created.Existing Contract updated. New version, same Contract ID.
TriggerNew deal in pipeline.Customer need mid-term.

How proration actually works
MN calculates from Effective Date through the next billing anchor. Example, on annual upfront billing:
Contract runs Jan 1 to Dec 31. Customer adds 10 seats on July 1. Annual seat price is $1,200.

Prorated charge = $1,200 × 10 seats × (184 / 365 days) = $6,049.

Invoice fires within the next billing cycle. Reference IDs point back to the amendment Quote, the original Contract, and the original Invoice for that period.
Key fields that change on amendment
Anchor
Contract ID stays the same. monetize_latest_quote_id updates to point to the amendment Quote.
Pipeline
monetize_deal_type = "amendment" or "upsell" on the new HubSpot Deal.
Forecast
Deal Amount = Incremental ARR, not Total ARR. Forecast does not double-count.
Effective Date
Drives proration math. Set this carefully. Editing it after signature requires a new amendment.
Billing
Adjustment invoice carries original Invoice reference for reconciliation against QBO postings.
One-Line Takeaway
Amendments start from the Contract, not Opportunity. MN calculates the delta, applies proration, keeps billing continuous.
Part 2 · Section 06 · Motion 3 of 3

Renewal Motion

Term extension at end of contract. Carry forward active products, apply uplift, start a new term the day the old one ends. Triggered by date rather than by customer need.

◆
Continuity. End date of an old Contract becomes the start date of a new one. MN auto-anchors this when a renewal processes cleanly. Late or manual processing is where gaps and overlaps come from.
Renewal vs Amendment
DimensionRenewalAmendment
TriggerContract end date approaching. Time-driven.Customer need mid-term. Demand-driven.
GoalSecure the next term. Maintain continuity.Modify scope of current term immediately.
Pricing basisEnding quantity, carry-forward, plus uplift if defined.Delta against current state. Proration applied.
Contract resultNew Contract for new term. Old one closed.Same Contract, updated in place.
Billing impactNew term invoice. New anchor.Adjustment invoice for the delta.
Most common errorGap or overlap between terms.Wrong Effective Date breaks proration.

How uplift works
Configured at the policy level, applied automatically on Renewal Quote creation. ENTR's default is set in MN under Renewal Logic.
Default uplift: 5% on the carry-forward Subscription pricing.
Override: AM can waive or adjust on Renewal Quote before sending.
Applied to: per-unit rate × current quantity. New optional products added at the renewal use the current rate card, not uplifted.

Why uplift is easier to waive than add: if policy is 0% and the AM wants to ask for 5%, that becomes a negotiation. If the policy is 5% and the AM wants to waive it, that's a goodwill gesture.
What carries forward (and what doesn't)
Carry forward
Active Subscriptions, their current quantities, Account and Contact roles.
Recalculated
Per-unit price (with uplift). Total Contract Value. New billing anchor based on renewal start date.
Reset
Contract ID is new. Billing schedule is rebuilt. Term length resets to whatever the renewal Quote specifies.
Does not carry
One-time products (implementation, training). These do not renew. Add explicitly if needed again.
Optional add-ons
Removed if not on the renewal Quote. AM must include explicitly to retain.
One-Line Takeaway
Renewals extend the contract forward. MN resets the term cleanly so billing continues without gaps or overlaps.

Part 3 · Section 08

Stripe in the Background

MN orchestrates every payment, retry, refund, and credit through Stripe via API. ENTR staff do not touch Stripe dashboard for billing operations. Stripe holds the payment rail; MN decides what runs on it.

◆
How this works. MN tells Stripe what to do, when to do it, and how many times to retry on failure. Stripe sends back what actually happened. Stripe data exists for compliance and audit. Every billing decision originates in MN.
What MN does in Stripe (without you lifting a finger)
Standard operations that run on their own:
  • Creates a Customer on first invoice
    When MN posts first invoice for an account, it creates the corresponding Stripe Customer record, attaches metadata that includes MN Account ID, and stores any payment method the customer added via the hosted page.
  • Charges per invoice when invoice posts
    MN calls Stripe with the amount, currency, customer ID, and reference (MN Invoice ID). Stripe attempts the charge against stored payment method.
  • Retries failed charges on a smart schedule
    If first attempt declines, MN orchestrates retries based on the failure reason. Soft declines retry within hours. Hard declines pause and trigger a dunning email to the billing contact.
  • Issues refunds against original charges
    When MN issues a Credit Memo that needs cash back, MN calls Stripe with the original charge ID. Refund executes. Stripe webhooks success, MN updates the Credit Memo to Refunded.
  • Listens for chargebacks and disputes
    If a customer disputes a charge, Stripe sends a webhook. MN flags the original Invoice, alerts Finance, and pauses any further automatic actions on that Invoice until resolved.
  • Updates payment method when customer rotates a card
    Stripe's account updater service notifies MN of card changes. Tokens swap automatically. Future charges use the updated method without ENTR or the customer doing anything.
What you should not do in Stripe
Doing any of these creates drift between Stripe and MN. MN will not know the action happened, so MN will not update the customer or the books.
  • Issue a refund directly in Stripe dashboard
    Stripe will process it, but MN will not know. The Invoice in MN still shows Paid, so Finance reports do not capture the credit, and QBO postings will be off. Always issue refunds via a Credit Memo in MN.
  • Manually create a Customer or charge in Stripe
    Stripe will accept it, but the charge has no Invoice ID and no Contract reference. It will not appear in Finance reports. Untraceable revenue.
  • Edit a Customer's metadata in Stripe
    MN's Account ID reference will get overwritten. Future sync attempts may fail to match.
  • Cancel a Subscription in Stripe (if one exists)
    ENTR does not use Stripe's native subscription product. MN owns subscription state. Stripe only sees individual charges. If a subscription record exists in Stripe at all, it was likely a test artifact.
Why MN is still the source of truth even though Stripe holds the money rail
Context belongs to the invoice
A Stripe charge of $4,000 means nothing without knowing it was the Q3 portion of a 12-month subscription for Pro tier with 40 seats. That context lives on Invoice in MN.
MN decides what to charge and when
Stripe does not know about proration, uplift, or contract terms. It just executes what MN tells it. If MN is wrong, Stripe is faithfully wrong.
Audit trail is one-to-one
Every Stripe charge is tagged with MN Invoice ID. Every MN Invoice records Stripe Charge ID. You can walk that chain in either direction.

Decision Tree · Which Motion?

A rep, AM, or CSM has a customer change to make. Is it a New Business deal, an Amendment to an active contract, or a Renewal of an expiring one? Four questions, three outcomes.

◆
Why motion matters. Motion determines the starting record, the math, the invoice type, and where value lands in forecast. Picking the wrong one is the most common source of billing errors and pipeline noise.
START HERE
Does a Contract already exist for this customer?
No
Motion 1
New Business
Start from a fresh Opportunity in HubSpot. Build full Quote in MN from scratch. Contract created on signature.
Yes
Is Contract currently active (mid-term)?
Yes · Mid-Term
Motion 2
Amendment
Open from active Contract in MN. Add, remove, or change Subscriptions. Proration calculated from Effective Date.
No · At End-of-Term
Is the customer extending the contract?
Yes
Motion 3
Renewal
Carry forward active Subscriptions. Apply uplift. New Contract starts the day the old one ends.
No
Off-Path
Churn or Expiry
Contract closes on its end date. No new motion. CS handles offboarding workflow. Final invoice and reconciliation in MN.
Edge cases worth knowing
  • Customer has multiple active Contracts
    A change applies to a specific Contract. Open Contract you intend to amend. Do not assume most recent is correct.
  • "They want to add a new product line that's totally separate"
    Still an Amendment if it sits inside the existing Contract term. Only a New Business deal if it's a separately-termed agreement with its own dates.
  • Customer wants to swap one product for another mid-term
    Amendment with a downgrade line (remove product A) and an upgrade line (add product B) on the same Quote. Single transaction, single proration.
  • Renewal is early (customer wants to extend before the window opens)
    Still a Renewal. AM can manually start the renewal flow. MN respects the existing end date as the new start date unless explicitly overridden.
  • Customer wants to renew with completely different terms
    Still a Renewal. Carry-forward is the default, but every line on Renewal Quote is editable before signature. Big changes work fine here.
  • Billing-only correction (typo in address, wrong PO number)
    Not any of the three motions. Edit Invoice directly in MN if not yet sent, or issue a Credit Memo and re-invoice if already sent.
Quick reference card
New Business
No prior Contract. Fresh Opp → Quote → Contract.
Origin: HubSpot Opportunity.
Amendment
Active Contract exists. Mid-term change.
Origin: MN Contract → Amend.
Renewal
Contract approaching or at end date. Extend forward.
Origin: MN Renewal Quote (auto or manual).
When in doubt
Ask: "Is the customer changing what they already have, or buying something net new?" If changing, it's an Amendment. If buying net new, it's New Business. If extending the same thing forward, it's a Renewal.
▲
The single most expensive error. Treating an Amendment as a New Business deal because building a fresh quote feels faster. Result: a parallel Contract, double-billing for the customer, double-counted forecast, and two AR balances for the same revenue. Active Contract + customer change = Amendment.
Part 4 · Section 11

Common Failure Modes

Recurring ways data drifts across systems. Each case lists symptom, root cause, impact, and fix. Grouped by motion, with a cross-system set at the end.

◆
Reading the cases. Skim for the symptom. Each fix is written so an operator can resolve without escalating, or escalate with the right context if admin intervention is needed.
New Business · 3 cases
Wrong Primary Quote
High Impact
Symptom
Rep created three Quotes during negotiation. Customer signed Quote C. But Quote A is still marked Primary. Deal Amount in HubSpot reflects Quote A's number, not what was actually sold.
Root cause
Primary flag was set early and never updated when the deal moved to a different Quote. HubSpot reports against Primary Quote; Contract reflects the signed Quote.
Impact
Forecast and Contract disagree. Commission report likely uses Primary Quote and underpays or overpays the rep. Pipeline numbers look off.
Fix Mark winning Quote as Primary in MN before processing. MN auto-updates Deal Amount on the change. As a habit, reps should flip Primary as soon as a Quote is sent for signature, not after signing.
Line Items Not Syncing to HubSpot
Medium Impact
Symptom
Deal Amount in HubSpot looks right, but the Line Items section is empty or shows wrong product splits. Product-level forecasting reports return nothing.
Root cause
"Sync Line Items to Deal" flag was disabled in MN configuration, or specific Products in catalog have sync turned off.
Impact
No product-level visibility in HubSpot. Reports that depend on Line Items return blank. Commissions calculated on product splits go wrong.
Fix Verify catalog-level sync flag in MN. Check affected Products for individual sync settings. Re-process Quote to push Line Items retroactively. Confirm in HubSpot Deal that Line Items appear.
Post-Signature Edits Change the Numbers
Medium Impact
Symptom
Finance billed $50,000, but Sales Commission report says $45,000 for the same deal. Both pulled from MN.
Root cause
Quote was edited in MN after the signature snapshot was taken. Commission snapshot froze at one number, billing reflects the edit.
Impact
Rep argues commission. Finance argues invoicing. Both are right, both are looking at different versions of the truth.
Fix Quotes lock on signature by default. If a post-signature change is needed, it must go through Amendment, not Quote edit. Re-train reps and admins. If Quote was edited in error, escalate to a CPQ Admin to restore from the signature snapshot.
Amendment · 3 cases
Double Billing After Amendment
High Impact
Symptom
Customer is billed for the new add-on AND for the original full contract again the following month. Customer flags it. Painful escalation.
Root cause
The change was processed as a new Quote (New Business motion) instead of as an Amendment to the active Contract. Two parallel Contracts now exist for the same customer.
Impact
Customer trust hit. Refund required for the duplicate. Books need correction across MN and QBO. AR is wrong.
Fix Close the duplicate Contract in MN with the same start date as today (so no charges accrue). Issue Credit Memos for any duplicate Invoices already posted. Process refunds through MN, which routes to Stripe. Always launch amendments from active Contract.
Incorrect Proration on a Mid-Cycle Change
High Impact
Symptom
Customer added 10 seats on the 15th of the month. Got billed for the full month instead of the partial days remaining.
Root cause
Effective Date on Amendment Quote was set to the start of the billing period instead of the actual change date.
Impact
Customer overpays. Reputation impact. Manual credit memo required to make whole.
Fix Issue a Credit Memo in MN for the difference between actual and prorated. Set Effective Date carefully on future amendments. MN calculates proration accurately from whatever Effective Date is set; the failure is operator input, not platform.
Delta vs Total Forecast Confusion
Medium Impact
Symptom
Pipeline report shows $1.2M for an Expansion Deal that's actually a $20K upsell. Forecast roll-up is dramatically inflated.
Root cause
HubSpot Deal Amount mapped to MN's Total ARR (full contract value) instead of Incremental ARR (just the upsell delta).
Impact
Sales leaders think pipeline is bigger than it is. Quota attainment looks wrong. Renewal pipeline double-counted on top of Amendment pipeline.
Fix Update field mapping in MN configuration so Amendment-type Deals map to Incremental ARR. New Business Deals continue to map to Total ARR. Validate that monetize_deal_type is correctly set on each Deal.
Renewal · 3 cases
Gap Between Contract Terms
High Impact
Symptom
Customer has three days of unbilled service between Contract A ending and Contract B starting. Or worse: service was cut off briefly before being restored.
Root cause
Renewal was processed late. Start date of new Contract was entered manually and didn't anchor to the previous end date.
Impact
Lost revenue for the gap days. Possible service interruption. Customer questions accuracy of the whole agreement.
Fix Set Renewal Window Alert at 90 days out (default in MN). On Renewal Quote creation, MN auto-anchors the new start date to the previous end date. If processed late, manually back-date the start to the day after Contract A ended. Don't leave it on default-today.
Overlapping Billing Across Renewals
High Impact
Symptom
Customer is billed twice for January. Old Contract still active, new Contract already started.
Root cause
Old Contract was not closed cleanly when the renewal processed. Manual date entry created an overlap window.
Impact
Customer overbilled. Refund needed. Books need cleanup across both MN and QBO.
Fix Process Renewal Quote through MN's standard flow, which auto-closes the prior Contract on its end date. If overlap already happened: void the duplicate Invoice in MN, issue Credit Memo, refund through MN. Audit Contract pair to confirm clean boundaries.
Uplift Missing or Wrong
Medium Impact
Symptom
Renewal Quote shows the same per-unit prices as the prior term. The expected 5% uplift is nowhere. Or the uplift is applied twice.
Root cause
Renewal pricing was overridden manually before sending. Or the renewal policy in MN doesn't have the uplift configured for relevant Offering.
Impact
Lost revenue across the renewal book. Or customer pushback on an unintentional double uplift.
Fix Verify Renewal Logic settings in MN: default uplift %, which Offerings it applies to. If pricing was manually overridden, AM should re-build Quote from carry-forward and only override after deliberate review.
Cross-System · payment and books
Stripe-Only Refund Causes QBO Drift
Medium Impact
Symptom
Stripe shows a $500 refund issued last week. MN Invoice still shows Paid. QBO shows nothing about the refund. Bank reconciliation breaks.
Root cause
Someone refunded directly from Stripe dashboard instead of issuing a Credit Memo in MN. MN never received the event in a form that updates Invoice.
Impact
Books out of sync. Customer-facing AR balance wrong. Audit risk. Finance has to manually adjust both MN and QBO.
Fix Manually issue a Credit Memo in MN for the same amount, with a note referencing the original Stripe refund. This corrects MN and triggers a QBO push to balance the books. Going forward: refund only via Credit Memo in MN, never directly in Stripe.
QBO Postings Drift From MN Reports
Medium Impact
Symptom
QBO month-end revenue total disagrees with the Monthly Revenue & Collections Summary in MN. Variance is small but recurring.
Root cause
Common patterns: a manual QBO entry made before MN report was pulled, a refund issued in Stripe directly that bypassed MN, or a credit memo issued in MN after close.
Impact
Reconciliation takes longer at close. AR aging in QBO drifts from reality in MN over time.
Fix Use MN reports as the authoritative figure for any period. Lock the workflow so refunds and credits originate in MN. If a Stripe-only refund slipped through, issue a matching Credit Memo in MN to bring reports back in line, then re-post in QBO.
●
For any drift across systems. Fix at source. Pricing or dates wrong, fix in MN. Pipeline stage wrong, fix in HubSpot. Ledger entry wrong, fix in QBO. Edits downstream while the source is still wrong create two sources of truth and twice the eventual cleanup.
